💥 The Big Bang — the expanding universe

In the 1920s Edwin Hubble found that almost every galaxy is rushing away from us — and the farther one is, the faster it flees. Rewind that expansion and everything converges: about 13.8 billion years ago the entire observable universe was packed into an unimaginably hot, dense point, and it has been expanding ever since. What the expansion tells us

Hubble's law

Recession speed is proportional to distance: a galaxy twice as far away recedes twice as fast. Watch the outer galaxies pull away fastest — that's the signature of uniform expansion.

Space itself stretches

Galaxies aren't flying through space from an explosion — the space between them is growing. The wave­lengths of their light get stretched too, shifting it red.

There is no center

Click any galaxy: every observer sees the same thing — everyone else rushing away from them. The Big Bang happened everywhere at once, not at one point in space.

The evidence

Three pillars: the redshift of galaxies, the leftover heat glow (the cosmic microwave background), and the exact ratio of hydrogen to helium the theory predicts.

The expansion isn't just continuing — it's accelerating, driven by a mysterious dark energy that makes up ~68% of the universe. What it is remains one of the biggest open questions in physics. The Big Bang explains the universe's history astonishingly well, yet what banged, and why, is still unknown.
