🗺️ Word Embeddings — meaning as geometry

Before a model can compute with language, it turns each word into a list of numbers — a point in space, called an embedding. Words used in similar ways end up near each other, so meaning becomes location. Even more striking: directions carry meaning too. The step from "man" to "woman" is the same step as "king" to "queen" — so you can literally do arithmetic on meaning: king − man + woman ≈ queen. Explore the map below, or run an analogy.

Why this is powerful

Similarity = closeness

Click any word: its nearest neighbours are its most related words. "cat" sits by "dog" and "lion"; "paris" by other capitals. Distance in this space is semantic similarity.

Meaning = direction

Consistent relationships become consistent vectors. "Gender," "capital-of," "plural," even "past tense" each point a fixed way — which is why the analogy arrows come out parallel.

Learned, not programmed

Nobody hand-places these; models learn them from raw text, on the principle that a word is defined by the company it keeps. Similar contexts ⇒ similar vectors.

The input to everything

Embeddings are the first layer of an LLM: text → vectors it can add, compare, and attend over. Good embeddings are why models grasp that "king" and "monarch" are close but "king" and "kangaroo" are not.

Real embeddings live in hundreds or thousands of dimensions (not 2), so far more relationships fit without collisions — this map is a hand-placed 2-D cartoon to make the idea tangible. But word2vec and every modern LLM really do solve "king − man + woman" and land on "queen."
