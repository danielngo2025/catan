🚀 Special Relativity — why moving clocks run slow

Einstein started from one stubborn fact: light always travels at the same speed, no matter how fast you're moving. Follow that to its conclusion and something has to give — and what gives is time itself. Below are two identical "light clocks," each ticking once per round trip of a photon between two mirrors. The top one is at rest; the bottom one moves. Because the moving photon has to travel a longer diagonal path at the same speed c, it ticks slower. Slide toward light speed and watch the gap explode.

The consequences

Nothing outruns light

The speed of light c is the universe's hard limit and is the same for every observer. You can add speed forever and only ever approach c, never reach it.

Time dilation

A moving clock ticks slower by the factor γ. At 87% of light speed γ = 2, so time runs at half rate; at 99.5% it runs 10× slow. Real muons and atomic clocks confirm it.

Length contraction

A moving object shrinks along its direction of motion by the same factor γ. Space and time trade off so that everyone still measures light at exactly c.

E = mc²

Mass and energy are the same thing. A tiny mass holds enormous energy (c² is a huge number) — the principle behind the Sun, nuclear power, and the bomb.

There's no experiment you could do inside the moving ship to tell you're moving — to you, your own clock ticks normally and it's the outside world that looks slow and contracted. Motion is relative; only the speed of light is absolute. GPS satellites apply these corrections (plus general-relativity ones) every day, or your maps would drift kilometres off within hours.
