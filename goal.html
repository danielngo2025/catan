🏭 The Goal — Theory of Constraints

Eli Goldratt's big idea: a system is only as fast as its slowest step. Raw material flows left→right through five stations. Each step, a station can process up to its capacity — but only if parts are waiting. Watch where inventory piles up, and who really sets the pace.

The Five Focusing Steps

  1. Identify the constraint — the one station that limits the whole line.
  2. Exploit it — make sure the constraint never sits idle or wastes effort.
  3. Subordinate everything else — other stations serve the constraint's pace, not their own local efficiency.
  4. Elevate the constraint — add capacity to it (drag its slider up).
  5. Repeat — once it's no longer the slowest, a new constraint appears. Go again.

Try it: find the red station, raise its capacity, and watch throughput climb — until a different station turns red. That's step 5 in action. Turn on dice mode to see why even a "balanced" line ships less than its average capacity: downstream stations starve whenever an upstream one has a bad roll.
