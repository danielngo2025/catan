🌌 General Relativity — gravity is curved spacetime

Newton said gravity is a force reaching across empty space. Einstein said there's no force at all: mass and energy bend spacetime, and things simply coast along the straightest available paths through that curved geometry. Below, a heavy mass warps the grid into a well. Launch a probe and watch it orbit — in Einstein mode the orbit slowly precesses (traces a rosette), a real effect Newton's physics can't produce. Crank the mass up and the well becomes a black hole.

The big ideas

Spacetime is the stage

Space and time are one flexible 4D fabric. It isn't a fixed backdrop — it stretches, curves, and ripples in response to what's in it.

Mass tells space how to curve…

"Matter tells spacetime how to curve; spacetime tells matter how to move." A freely-falling object feels no force — it just follows the straightest line (a geodesic) through curved space.

The precession test

Mercury's orbit precesses 43 arc-seconds per century more than Newton allows. Einstein's curvature predicted exactly that in 1915 — the first triumph of the theory.

Light bends too

Even massless light follows curved spacetime, so gravity deflects starlight and forms lensing rings. Confirmed in the 1919 eclipse that made Einstein famous.

Push the mass high enough and spacetime curves so steeply that not even light can climb back out: a black hole, ringed by an event horizon — the point of no return. Time itself runs slower in strong gravity (gravitational time dilation), which is why GPS satellites must correct their clocks to stay accurate. Same theory, everyday consequence.
