📈 Kaizen — the power of tiny gains

Kaizen (改善, "change for the better") is the practice of continuous, incremental improvement — many small steps instead of rare big leaps. It sounds modest, but the math is not: a 1% improvement repeated every day makes you ~37× better in a year, while slipping 1% a day leaves you at almost nothing. How kaizen actually runs: the PDCA loop

Plan a small change→ Do it on a small scale→ Check the result→ Act: keep or drop it ↺ then run the loop again
Small steps

Improvements so small they're almost impossible to fail — and easy to repeat forever.

Everyone, every day

The people doing the work suggest the fixes; improvement is part of the job, not a project.

Eliminate waste (muda)

Hunt the small frictions: waiting, rework, extra steps. Remove one, then the next.

Standardize the win

When a change works, make it the new normal — so you improve from it, not back to it.

This is why kaizen beats the big-bang rewrite: a giant push is risky, hard to reverse, and happens once. A steady 1% is low-risk, self-correcting, and — as the chart shows — compounds into something a single leap can't match.
