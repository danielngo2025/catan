🌡️ Thermodynamics — entropy & the arrow of time

The most famous law of thermodynamics — the Second — says the entropy (disorder) of an isolated system only ever goes up. It's why heat flows from hot to cold, why your coffee cools but never spontaneously reheats, and why the past is different from the future. Below, all the gas starts crammed in the left half. The four laws (in plain English)

0th — Equilibrium

If A and B are each in thermal balance with C, they're in balance with each other. This is what lets a thermometer work.

1st — Energy is conserved

You can't create or destroy energy, only move or convert it. Heat + work in = change in internal energy. No free lunch.

2nd — Entropy increases

Isolated systems drift toward disorder. Heat flows hot→cold on its own; the reverse needs work. This is the arrow of time (shown above).

3rd — Absolute zero

As temperature approaches 0 K, entropy approaches a minimum — and you can never quite reach 0 K in finite steps.

Why won't the gas ever cram itself back into the left half on its own? It could — but the odds of all N particles being on one side at once are about 1 in 2N. For just 200 particles that's 1 in ~1060: you'd wait far longer than the age of the universe. Entropy doesn't rise because it's a law of force — it rises because disorder is overwhelmingly more likely than order.
