🌍 Newton's Law of Gravity

In 1687 Newton claimed the same force that drops an apple holds the Moon in its orbit — and that every mass pulls every other mass with a force that weakens with the square of the distance. Play with both ideas below.

2 · Falling forever — orbits

A planet in orbit is just falling while moving sideways fast enough to keep missing the star. Set the launch speed and watch what happens.

Speed is shown as a % of the perfect-circle speed. Under 100% → an ellipse that dips inward; near 141% → fast enough to escape forever. And try the planet-mass slider: the orbit's shape doesn't change — heavy and light objects fall the same way.

The apple and the Moon

Newton's leap: fire a cannonball harder and harder and it lands farther away — until it's fast enough that the Earth curves away beneath it as fast as it falls. Now it never lands. That's an orbit. The Moon is doing exactly this, and the force pulling it is the very same gravity that drops the apple — just weaker, because the Moon is ~60× farther from Earth's center (so the pull is ~60² ≈ 3600× weaker).
