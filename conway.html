🔀 Conway's Law

Any organization that designs a system will produce a design whose structure is a copy of the organization's communication structure. — Melvin Conway, 1967

In plain terms: you ship your org chart. The boundaries between your teams become the boundaries between your software modules, and two modules only get a clean interface if the teams behind them actually talk. What this means for you

Conway's Law isn't advice — it's an observation that keeps coming true. If three teams build one product, you'll almost always get three services with awkward seams exactly where the teams didn't coordinate. A single big team tends to grow a single big codebase (a monolith).

The Inverse Conway Maneuver: since the org shapes the architecture, flip it around — decide the architecture you want, then reorganize the teams to match. Want loosely-coupled microservices? Create small, independent teams with clear ownership. Want a tightly integrated core? Keep the people who build it talking constantly.
