🎨 Diffusion Models — pictures from noise

AI image generators — DALL·E, Stable Diffusion, Midjourney — don't paint. They denoise. In training they take real images and progressively add random noise until nothing's left, learning to undo each step. To generate, they start from a screen of pure static and run that learned "un-noising" over and over, and a coherent image emerges from the chaos — steered by your text prompt. Press Generate to watch noise resolve into a picture, or drag the slider to scrub the timeline.

How it actually works

Forward: add noise

Take a real image and add a little Gaussian noise, again and again, until it's indistinguishable from static. This "forward process" is easy — and it creates the training data for the hard part.

Reverse: learn to undo it

A neural network (a U-Net) is trained to look at a noisy image and predict the noise to remove. Run it repeatedly and you walk backwards from static to a clean image — the "reverse process" you see above.

Generate from nothing

Since the model can denoise any static, you can hand it fresh random noise and it will hallucinate a brand-new, never-seen image out of it. Different noise ⇒ different picture.

Steered by the prompt

Your text is turned into vectors that nudge every denoising step toward images matching the words. That's how "an astronaut riding a horse" pulls the noise toward exactly that — guidance, not painting.

This demo fakes the denoiser — it simply blends a fixed target picture back in as the noise fades, so the reveal looks like real diffusion. A true model has no target; it predicts the noise from scratch at every step using billions of learned parameters. But the core idea — generation = repeated denoising from random static — is exactly right.
