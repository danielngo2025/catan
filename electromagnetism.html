🧲 Electromagnetism — electricity, magnetism & light are one thing

In the 1860s James Clerk Maxwell wrote four equations that tied electricity and magnetism into a single force. They had a stunning consequence: a changing electric field creates a magnetic field, whose change creates an electric field, and so on — a self-sustaining ripple that flies through empty space at one fixed speed. That speed turned out to be the speed of light. Light is an electromagnetic wave. Below, the red E field and blue B field oscillate at right angles; slide the frequency to travel the whole spectrum.

What Maxwell showed

One force, not two

Electricity and magnetism are two faces of the same thing. A moving charge makes a magnetic field; a changing magnetic field pushes charges. Maxwell unified them in four equations.

Fields make fields

A changing E field induces a B field and vice versa. Once started, the pair regenerates each other and propagates on its own — no medium required, straight through vacuum.

Light falls out of the math

The equations force the wave to travel at c ≈ 300,000 km/s. Maxwell realized that matched light's measured speed — so light must be an EM wave. A theory predicting what light is.

One spectrum

Radio, microwave, infrared, visible, UV, X-ray, gamma — all the same wave, differing only in frequency. Visible light is a razor-thin slice your eyes happen to detect.

Higher frequency means shorter wavelength and more energy per photon (E = hf) — which is why gamma rays are dangerous and radio waves are harmless. Maxwell's unification was the template for all of physics that followed: the electroweak theory later merged electromagnetism with the weak force, and the dream of unifying all four forces continues today.
