🧩 The Standard Model of particle physics

Everything you've ever touched is built from just a handful of elementary particles — and every push and pull between them comes from four forces. The Standard Model is the theory that catalogs them: 17 particles that, so far, appear to have no smaller parts. How to read it

Matter: 12 fermions

Six quarks and six leptons (spin ½). Quarks clump into protons and neutrons; the electron is a lepton. Everyday matter uses only the first generation: up, down, electron, and its neutrino.

Three generations

The columns repeat the same pattern three times, each heavier and less stable than the last. Nobody knows why there are exactly three — one of the model's deepest open questions.

Forces: 4 gauge bosons

Forces are carried by particles (spin 1): the gluon (strong), the photon (electromagnetic), and the W and Z (weak). Toggle a force above to see who feels it.

The Higgs

The lone spin-0 particle, confirmed at CERN in 2012. Particles gain mass by coupling to its field — the stronger the coupling, the heavier the particle (why the top quark is so massive).

The Standard Model is one of the most precisely tested theories in science — yet it's known to be incomplete. It says nothing about gravity, offers no particle for dark matter, and can't explain why the universe is made of matter rather than antimatter. The next big theory has to include everything here and then some.
