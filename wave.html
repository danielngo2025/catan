〰️ The Wave Equation — one rule for every wave

Strings, sound, light, water ripples, earthquakes — astonishingly, they all obey the same equation. It says the acceleration of each point is proportional to how curved the wave is there: sharp bends snap back hard. That single rule makes disturbances travel at a fixed speed c, pass through each other, and bounce off boundaries. Click anywhere on the string below to pluck it — or strike a harmonic.

Harmonics:

What the equation tells you

One equation, every medium

Only the speed c changes: it's set by the medium's stiffness and density (tension vs. mass for a string). Sound in air, light in vacuum, ripples on a pond — same maths, different c.

A pluck splits in two

Because the equation is symmetric, any initial shape splits into two half-height copies travelling opposite ways at speed c (d'Alembert's solution). Watch them separate, then recombine after reflecting.

Superposition

The equation is linear, so waves simply add and pass straight through one another unchanged. Two pulses cross and emerge intact — the reason many conversations can share one room of air.

Standing waves = music

Fixed ends permit only whole numbers of half-wavelengths — the harmonics. Their frequencies are the notes of a guitar or piano string; the fundamental plus overtones give an instrument its tone.

At a fixed end the wave flips and reflects (you'll see pulses invert). The very fact that light travels at a fixed speed c comes from Maxwell's equations rearranging into exactly this wave equation — which is how physicists first realized light is a wave. From guitars to fibre-optics, it's the same beautiful line of maths.
