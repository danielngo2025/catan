🧠 The Feynman Technique

Richard Feynman's test for real understanding: if you can't explain it simply, you don't understand it yet. Work a concept through these four steps — the clarity meter scores your explanation the way a curious 12-year-old would. Your notes save automatically in this browser.

1 · Study→ 2 · Teach it simply→ 3 · Find the gaps→ 4 · Simplify ↺ then repeat until it's obvious

1 Pick a concept

Name one thing you want to truly understand.

2 Explain it in plain words

Pretend you're teaching a smart 12-year-old. No jargon, short sentences, everyday words.

Write something to see your clarity score.

3 Mark the gaps

Where did you stumble, hand-wave, or fall back on jargon? Those are the spots to restudy from the source.

4 Simplify & make an analogy

Rewrite the hard parts. A good analogy ("it's like…") is the strongest sign you really get it.

See a worked example — explaining gravity the Feynman way

Concept: Why doesn't the Moon fall down?
Plain words: The Moon is falling — it keeps missing the Earth. It's moving sideways so fast that as it falls, the ground curves away beneath it just as much. So it falls forever in a circle.
Gap: Why does moving sideways make it "miss"? → restudy how a thrown ball's path curves, then throw it faster.
Analogy: It's like a cannonball fired so hard it goes all the way around the world and comes up behind you. Try it in the gravity simulator →
