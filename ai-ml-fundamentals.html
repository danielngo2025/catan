HOW A MODEL READS

Tokenization

Before a model can read, it chops your text into tokens — chunks that are often whole words, but rare words shatter into pieces.

  • IdeaModels never see letters or words — only token IDs (numbers).
  • Try itType a sentence and watch it split; see "tokenization" break into subwords.
  • WhyToken count is your cost and context-window limit.

REPRESENTING MEANING

Word Embeddings

Each token becomes a point in space, where distance is meaning — similar words sit close together.

  • IdeaMeaning becomes geometry: king − man + woman ≈ queen.
  • Try itExplore the map; watch analogies show up as parallel arrows.
  • WhyThis is how a model "knows" cat and dog are related.

UNDERSTANDING CONTEXT

Attention

Attention lets every word look at the other words to figure out what it means in context — the "T" in GPT.

  • IdeaThe breakthrough behind transformers — how "it" finds what it refers to.
  • Try itClick a word and see which earlier words it attends to.
  • WhyContext is everything: bank by a river vs a vault.

HOW A MODEL WRITES

Next-Token Prediction

An LLM writes one token at a time — predict the likeliest next token, add it, repeat. That's the whole trick.

  • IdeaIt's autocomplete on steroids — no plan, just the next token.
  • Try itSlide temperature from greedy to wild and sample a sentence.
  • WhyTemperature is the "creativity" dial you feel in every chatbot.
▶ Live demo /nexttoken.html

THE BUILDING BLOCK

The Perceptron

Underneath it all is the neuron: weighted inputs, summed and squashed into an output. Stack millions → a deep network.

  • IdeaOne neuron draws a single line between two classes.
  • Try itWatch it tune its weights to separate two groups — then hit the XOR wall.
  • WhyThat XOR limit is why we stack neurons into deep nets.

HOW A MODEL LEARNS

Gradient Descent

Learning = rolling downhill. Measure the error, then nudge every weight in the direction that lowers it — over and over.

  • IdeaFind the bottom of a valley you can only feel underfoot.
  • Try itTune the learning rate: converge, overshoot, or get stuck.
  • WhyThis one algorithm trains virtually every modern model.

BONUS · THE BIG QUESTION

Can it create something new?

Yes — but there are two kinds of "new." Predicting the next token isn't the same as copying.

  • YesAlmost every output is literally novel — a fresh point in an astronomically large space, not a lookup.
  • ButPure next-token models interpolate near what they've seen; they rarely leap outside it.
  • SoReal breakthroughs come from generate + verify — AlphaGo's move 37, faster algorithms, new proteins.

It's a creativity engine, not a copy machine.
